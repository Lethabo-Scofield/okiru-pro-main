# ICT Generic: our scorecard against the Empower toolkit

For the sector expert. This compares our ICT Generic scorecard with the Empower
"BBBEE Toolkit (ICT Generic) Template v1.4", recalculated by Excel itself, on
sixteen invented companies. No client data was used.

The rule we worked to: the spreadsheet is the reference. Where one of its
formulas is plainly broken, we do not copy the break. We score what the
formula evidently means, using the spreadsheet's own figures, and list the
fault below so you can rule on it.

## Result

**834 of 834 compared figures agree** across the sixteen companies: on each
company, 36 indicator lines, the 7 element totals, the grand total, the
achieved level and the discounted level (736 figures), and the recognised
amount of each of the 98 ESD contributions.

Every scorecard figure agrees to within half a cent of a point, and every
recognised amount to the cent. On the lines listed under
"Toolkit faults" the comparison is against what the faulty formula evidently
means, worked out from the spreadsheet's own figures, not against the number
the spreadsheet shows.

The corrected totals are mostly higher, because two lines the spreadsheet
always scores at 0 (designated groups and new entrants) now count. They can
also be lower: netting acquisition debt for the black share of a stake only
lowers net value, and in company 109 it takes net value to 3.10, under its 3.2
sub-minimum, so the level is discounted where the spreadsheet does not
discount it.

The sixteen companies were built to exercise every input a scored line reads:
full and small companies, a company with no black ownership (net value fails
its sub-minimum), a well-transformed company at Level 1, a loss-making year
with no ESD or SED spend, disabled-learner spend with and without the EAP
split, training-manager salary and overhead with disabled staff in every
group, beneficiaries creating jobs in both bonus tiers, graduations, minority
investments, first-time suppliers, Supplier Development and three-year
contract suppliers, a procurement buffer and TMPS exclusions.

The two "jobs" companies (108 and 115) always carry, in both Supplier and
Enterprise Development, a loan at a lower interest rate and three
shorter-payment contributions: one paid within 14 days (in each company one of
them exactly on day 14), one paid after 14 days and one with no invoice or
payment dates. Three of the four loans are below prime; company 115's
Enterprise Development loan is above it (fault 10 below). Company 115 also has
an investment at a lower dividend. The template's Enterprise
Development list does not offer "Shorter payment periods"; those rows show
what its formulas do with that value typed or pasted in, which our ESD
register accepts for either element.

An element already at its maximum points hides how it got there, so every ESD
contribution is also compared on its own: the template's recognised amount for
the row against ours, to the cent.

## What the toolkit does, which we now do as well

These are the template's own choices. Our ICT Generic scorecard now follows
each of them.

- **Ownership.** Each shareholder is weighed by the value of the holding.
  Designated-group and new-entrant shares are read per shareholder. Each black
  shareholder's acquisition debt is netted against the company value. Net value
  is that debt-netted black economic interest measured against 100%, for 8
  points. Targets: voting rights 30% (4 points), black women's voting rights
  10% (2), economic interest 30% (4), black women's economic interest 10% (2),
  designated groups 3% (3), new entrants 2% (2).
- **Management control.** Board members are counted by head, not by voting
  rights. Senior, middle and junior management are measured against the
  provincial EAP split. Points: board 3 and 2, executive directors 2 and 1,
  other executive management 3 and 2, senior 2 and 1, middle 2 and 1, junior 1
  and 1, employees with disabilities 2. There is no option to combine other
  executive and senior management in this template.
- **Skills development.** Five lines, each split across the six black groups
  by effective EAP, so a group can earn at most its share of the points:
  - learning programmes: 6% of the leviable amount, 8 points. Course costs of
    categories A to E and stipends of categories B, C and D count in full.
    Category F and G course costs and all other costs (travel, accommodation,
    catering, stationery, facilities) share one cap of 15% of all training
    spend. 15% of the training manager's salary and the training overhead is
    shared out by each group's share of the workforce;
  - disabled learners: 0.3% of the leviable amount, 4 points. Without the
    verifier's sign-off the black learners with disabilities are measured as
    one pool against the whole target; with it, by EAP group;
  - black learners in learnerships, apprenticeships and internships (categories
    B, C and D): 2.5% of headcount, 4 points;
  - the unemployed ones among them: 2.5% of headcount, 4 points;
  - absorption (bonus): absorbed unemployed black learners who completed,
    against all unemployed learners who completed (100% target), 5 points.
  There is no bursary line in this template.
- **Preferential procurement.** The "all suppliers" line counts every
  supplier's recognised spend; the "Empowering supplier?" answer is never read.
  QSE and EME lines go by the supplier's current size. The 51% black-owned line
  has a 40% target for 9 points. A QSE or EME supplier at least 51% black owned
  earns the 1.2 recognition factor when it is a Supplier Development
  beneficiary, holds a three-year contract or is a first-time supplier.
- **Enterprise and supplier development.** A contribution is recognised at its
  benefit factor whatever the beneficiary's size or ownership (see the
  questions below). Guarantees count at 50%. A loan below prime and an
  investment at a lower dividend count at prime less the actual rate.
  Shorter payment terms count at 15% less one point per day taken to pay,
  worked out for every such contribution: nothing after 14 days, and nothing
  without both an invoice and a payment date (our other scorecards give an
  undated one the table's 15%). Supplier Development is 2% of NPAT for
  10 points; Enterprise Development 3% of NPAT for 15 points, plus 1 bonus point
  for a graduation and a jobs bonus of 1 point (jobs up to 10% of a
  beneficiary's workforce) or 2 points (11% or more).
- **Socio-economic development.** Only contributions marked as ICT-specific
  initiatives count: 1.5% of NPAT for 12 points.
- **Sub-minimums.** Net value at least 3.2 points, Skills at least 8 of its 20
  base points, Procurement at least 10 of its 25 base points, Supplier
  Development at least 4 points and Enterprise Development at least 6 of its 15
  base points. Missing any one drops the level by one; a discounted Level 8 is
  Non-compliant.
- **Levels.** 120 points for Level 1, then 115, 110, 100, 95, 90, 75 and 55.

## Toolkit faults (not copied)

Each one: where it is, what it does, and what we do instead.

1. **Designated groups and new entrants always score 0** (Ownership Scorecard,
   cells H10 and H12). The points are worked out against the target cut to
   its first three characters, so 3% and 2% become zero and the division
   fails. Same fault as the RCOGP Generic toolkit. We measure against the full
   3% and 2%.
2. **Acquisition debt is netted differently by row** (the "Oustanding Debts
   Calcs" sheet, spelled so in the workbook, feeding economic interest and net
   value). The first indebted
   shareholder is netted one way and every later one another way, and an
   indebted shareholder's whole stake is counted as black. Same sheet as
   RCOGP Generic. We net every shareholder the first row's way, for the black
   share of the stake only.
3. **A Supplier Development minority investment is recognised at 0** (ESD and
   SED Calcs, the Supplier Development table, row 9). The table holds the
   minority-investment row twice and the lookup finds the copy with no
   factor. The Enterprise Development table says 70%. Same fault as RCOGP
   Generic. We use 70%.
4. **Employed learners count as unemployed** (Skills Calcs, cells F58 to F62,
   the "unemployed learners in learnerships" line). Only the African-male row
   checks that "Employed?" is No; the other five groups count every black
   learner in a learnership, apprenticeship or internship. We count unemployed
   learners only, in every group.
5. **Training-admin costs on the disabled line go to African men only**
   (Employee Demographics, cells J7 to N7). These cells should hold each
   group's share of disabled black staff. They were copied across with
   references that drifted onto unrelated columns, and read 0. So only African
   men receive a share of the training manager's salary and overhead on the
   disabled-learner line. We give every group its share of disabled black
   staff, as the African-male cell does.
6. **A first-time supplier never earns the 1.2 factor on that ground**
   (Procurement Data, the calculated "First-time supplier?" column). When no
   scenario answer is entered it reads the three-year-contract answer instead
   of the first-time-supplier answer. Every other calculated column beside it
   (size, level, ownership, Supplier Development recipient, three-year
   contract) falls back to its own answer. We read the first-time-supplier
   answer.
7. **Both job-creation tiers can score together** (ESD Scorecard, cells H16 and
   H17). A company with one beneficiary under 10% and another over 11% gets 1 +
   2 = 3 points, so Enterprise Development can reach 19 although the element is
   weighted 18 (15 + 1 + 2) and the gap column treats the tiers as
   alternatives. We award the higher tier only.
8. **A total exactly on a threshold drops a level** (Scorecard Calculations,
   cell C12). The level lookup takes the highest threshold strictly below the
   total, so exactly 100 points is Level 5, not Level 4. Same fault as RCOGP
   Generic. We give the level the total reaches.
9. **The level is taken from the projected column, not the actual points**
   (Scorecard Calculations, cells C3 to C10, read by the Summary's achieved and
   discounted levels). With no scenario entered the two columns should agree,
   but two projected formulas differ from their actual twins:
   - absorption: the projected denominator (Skills Calcs E76) adds the absorbed
     learners' total (Skills Toolkit L154) where its actual twin adds the
     manual-input total (L142), so projected absorption is lower;
   - disabled learners without the EAP split: the projected cost cap (Skills
     Calcs W31 to W36) is scaled by each group's EAP share and the actual one
     (O31 to O36) is not.
   On twelve of the sixteen companies the projected total was 0.1 to 1.0
   points below the actual one. On company 113 that alone costs a level: the
   spreadsheet's own actual total is 115.06 (Level 2) but its projected total
   is 114.72, so it shows Level 3. We give the level of the actual points shown
   on the scorecard.
10. **A loan above prime is recognised at a negative amount** (ESD Data, the
   "Lower interest Rate / Lower Dividend Rate" column). The factor is the prime
   rate less the actual rate, with no floor, so a loan charged above prime
   subtracts from the other contributions of its element: in company 115 a loan
   of R324,218.42 at 12% against a prime of 10.75% is recognised at minus
   R4,052.73. The same formula reads a blank actual rate as 0%, so a loan
   whose rate was never entered counts at the full prime rate. A contribution
   cannot be worth less than nothing, and a missing rate is not a rate. We
   floor the factor at 0 and recognise the row once both rates are entered.
   Investments at a lower dividend use the same column. (Company 115's
   Enterprise Development is at its maximum either way, so only its
   row-by-row comparison shows this.)

## Questions for you (we follow the spreadsheet until you rule)

- **ESD beneficiaries are not checked.** The template works out whether each
  beneficiary qualifies (a 51% black-owned EME or QSE, and for Enterprise
  Development an ICT company), but every branch of its "recognised amount"
  formula gives the full amount times the benefit factor. So the "ICT
  Company?" answer and the beneficiary's size and ownership change nothing.
  The RCOGP Generic toolkit does exclude non-qualifying beneficiaries. We
  follow this template (no check). Should non-qualifying beneficiaries count?
- **Employee time in ESD.** The template has a branch that divides employee
  time by 160 (hours in a month), but it can never be reached, so employee time
  counts at the full amount times 60%. We follow that.
- **Disabled learners without the EAP split.** Each of the six groups is
  allowed category F and G and other costs up to 15% of all training spend,
  rather than one 15% cap for the whole pool. The cap is rarely reached; we
  follow the actual column.
- **Absorption.** The denominator counts every unemployed learner who
  completed, of any race and including foreign nationals, while only black
  learners can be absorbed for points. We follow that.
- **Blank choices score 0.** The template ships with "Actual or projected
  spend?" (Procurement Scorecard E6) and "EAP targets applicable on disability
  spend?" (Skills Toolkit D9) blank, which scores every procurement line and the
  disabled line 0 until someone picks. Our comparison picks "Actual" and, for
  most companies, "No".

## Where our model needed a new answer

Three inputs the toolkit reads had no place in our calculator, so we added
them. Each of these three rules applies only once its answer is captured. The
workbook, the screen consultants fill in, now captures all three for ICT
Generic:

- **ICT-specific initiative** (SED). The workbook already asked this
  ("ICT-Specific?"), but its answer never reached the calculator; now it does.
  Only an answer counts: "No" removes the contribution, as in the template,
  while a blank, or a contribution entered somewhere that never asks, still
  counts. Here we part from the template, which scores a blank as 0, so that
  contributions entered before the question mattered are not dropped without
  anyone being asked. Should a blank count? The spreadsheet import now reads a
  sheet's own "ICT Specific Initiative?" column, and no longer writes "No" on
  every row it brings in.
- **First-time supplier** (procurement). A new "First-time Supplier?" column on
  the supplier register, shown for ICT Generic only. Unanswered means No, which
  is safe because the answer can only add the 1.2 factor.
- **The verification agent's sign-off on the EAP split for disabled learners**
  (Skills). A new tick box in the Skills details, shown for ICT Generic only.
  Unticked, the disabled line is one pool against the whole target.

Two things to check before this goes live:

- Rows brought in by the spreadsheet import before this change carry a "No"
  the importer wrote itself, and the workbook shows them as "No". That "No" is
  now scored, so those rows stop counting until someone changes it to Yes. Any
  ICT Generic client whose SED came in through the import should be looked at
  before release.
- Pasting into the "ICT-Specific?" column turns a blank cell into "No", as the
  paste does for every Yes/No column. The cell then shows "No", so the change
  is visible, but it is scored.

The Toolkit's own pillar pages do not show these three questions yet, and the
workbook's Excel export does not write the first-time-supplier answer.

One more capture gap, outside these three and in every sector: the prime and
actual interest rates entered in the workbook do not yet reach the
calculator, so in the live app a loan below prime or an investment at a lower
dividend is recognised at 0. The comparison above enters the rates directly.
It is filed as a separate fix.

## Not compared

The toolkit's scenario, manual-override and prior-year columns, the manual
totals for jobs created and beneficiaries' workforce (ESD Scorecard D4 to D8),
and the YES initiative were left blank in every case: our scorecard has no
equivalent of the scenario and manual columns, and YES points never change the
B-BBEE level. In the manual columns the template also has three shifted
references (Skills Calcs L34 to L36 read the manual cell one column to the
left of their own group's), which matter only if someone types into them.
