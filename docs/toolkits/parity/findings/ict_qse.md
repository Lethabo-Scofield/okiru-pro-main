# ICT QSE: our scorecard against the Empower toolkit

For: Chengetai (sector expert). Written in plain English; no code.

Toolkit checked: **BBBEE Toolkit (ICT QSE) Template v1.1** (the Empower workbook in `docs/toolkits`).
Method: invented companies (no client data) were typed into the real workbook, Excel recalculated it, and every
line of its Summary Scorecard was compared with what our system scores for the same company, to the cent of a point.

## What our system now does the toolkit's way

These are the rules this toolkit uses for ICT QSE. Our ICT QSE scorecard now follows each of them. Where the rule
is a choice the Codes could be read differently on, it is marked **(please confirm)**.

1. **Ownership.** Shareholders are weighed by the value of their shares. Acquisition debt is netted shareholder by
   shareholder. The net value line is measured against 100% of black economic interest after debt, with full
   points at 100%. The single QSE line "black new entrants **or** designated groups" adds the two groups'
   economic interest together and measures it against 2% for 3 points.
2. **Management control, combined layout.** When the client chooses to combine executive and senior management,
   senior managers are counted on the two executive lines (50% / 25% targets), and the senior, middle and junior
   lines count middle and junior managers only (60% / 30%). Without the combination, executive directors and
   other executive managers make the executive lines, and senior, middle and junior managers together make the
   other two. Semi-skilled and unskilled staff are in neither.
3. **Skills development.** There is no EAP split in the QSE toolkit. Each spend line takes the course costs of
   categories A to E and the stipends of categories B to D for the learners concerned. It adds their F and G course
   costs and "other" costs (travel, accommodation and so on), but those two together may not exceed 15% of all
   training spend. It also adds 15% of the training manager's salary and training overheads, shared by the matching
   share of the workforce. Targets are 3% (black people, 15 points), 1% (black women, 7 points) and 0.15% (black
   people with disabilities, 3 points) of the leviable amount. The leviable amount is the larger of the SDL × 100
   and the total payroll.
4. **Absorption bonus (please confirm).** The toolkit gives the 5 bonus points by the **black share of the absorbed
   learners**. It counts the unemployed learners who completed a learnership, apprenticeship or internship and were
   absorbed, and asks what share of them is black. It does **not** compare the number absorbed with the number who
   completed. So one black learner absorbed out of fifty who completed earns all 5 points. We now score it the same
   way, because that is what the spreadsheet does. This reading is unusual, and we need your ruling on it.
5. **Preferential procurement.** The all-suppliers line counts every supplier's recognised spend. The toolkit never
   reads the "Empowering supplier?" answer. A QSE or EME supplier that is at least 51% black owned earns the 1.2
   recognition factor if it is a supplier-development beneficiary, holds a 3-year contract, or is a first-time
   supplier. Lines are measured against the larger of the TMPS and the supplier listing plus any buffer.
6. **Supplier and enterprise development targets (please confirm).** The toolkit sets both at **1% of NPAT** (5 points
   each). Our ICT QSE settings had 2% from the July gazette review (AICT604). The spreadsheet now rules. The ED
   sub-minimum is 40% of its 5-point base line.
7. **ESD beneficiaries are not tested.** The toolkit works out a "Qualify?" answer for each ESD contribution, using
   the beneficiary's size, black ownership and "ICT company?" answer. But both branches of its recognised-amount
   formula give the same amount, so the answer never changes a score. We do not test the beneficiary either.
8. **SED.** Only contributions marked as ICT-specific initiatives count. The percentage benefiting black people is not
   used. Target: 1% of NPAT for 12 points.
9. **Deemed NPAT.** NPAT is the larger of the current year and the average of the current and four prior years. If
   that is below a quarter of the industry norm times revenue, the toolkit uses the larger of (smallest qualifying
   margin × revenue) and (quarter-norm × revenue). A manual deemed NPAT always wins.
10. **Discounting.** The toolkit discounts a QSE by one level when **any** priority sub-minimum fails: net value
    (3.2 of 8), Skills (10 of the 25 base points), Procurement (8 of the 20 base points), SD (2 of 5) or ED (2 of 5).
    This is stricter than the Codes' QSE rule, which is Ownership plus either Skills or ESD. **(please confirm)**
    A discounted Level 8 becomes Non-compliant.
